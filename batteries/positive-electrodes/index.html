Chapter 13

Positive electrode materials: layered oxides, spinels and olivine phosphates

Why are today's cells built on NMC or LFP, and what comes next?

The other side: positive electrodes

So far we have changed only the negative electrode. Let us now cross to the other side, because in today’s cells it is the positive electrode that holds the cell back: its capacity lags behind that of the negative electrode, and its materials account for around half the cost of a cell.1

Almost every commercial positive electrode belongs to one of three families of oxide, identified in Goodenough’s group in the 1980s: layered oxides such as LiCoO2, spinels such as LiMn2O4, and polyanion oxides such as LiFePO4. Oxides rather than sulfides, because the top of the oxide-ion band lies lower in energy, which lets a cell reach about 4 V instead of under 2.5 V.2

The three differ in how the lithium moves and how tightly the oxygen is held. In a layered oxide, lithium moves in planes between sheets of transition-metal oxide; these materials conduct well and pack densely. The spinel gives lithium three-dimensional paths, which favours power, but in practice it is limited to LiMn2O4, whose manganese dissolves and poisons the graphite.2 In a polyanion oxide, the strongly covalent P–O or S–O bonds weaken the covalency of the metal–oxygen bonds, lower the energy of the metal’s redox couple and so raise the voltage, the inductive effect: Fe2+/Fe3+ works below 2.5 V in Fe2O3 but at 3.6 V in Fe2(SO4)3. Oxygen held by phosphorus makes these materials thermally stable and safe, and iron makes them abundant; but they conduct poorly, so their particles must be small and coated with carbon, and they pack less densely.2

From LiCoO2 to nickel-rich NMC

LiCoO2 gives up only about half its lithium, because beyond that the Co3+/Co4+ band overlaps the top of the oxide band and oxygen leaves the lattice.2 Replacing cobalt with nickel and manganese gives NMC, LiNi1−y−zMnyCozO2, and each metal brings something. Nickel brings the capacity: it can be oxidized from Ni2+ all the way to Ni4+ without much loss of oxygen. Manganese, as Mn4+, stabilizes the structure without taking part in the redox. Cobalt keeps the layers ordered and the kinetics fast; in NCA, aluminium in place of manganese adds thermal stability.2,3

More nickel means that more lithium can be taken out within the same voltage window,4 and NMC811 gives about 200 mAh/g.1 The price is stability. Ni4+ is unstable against the electrolyte; nickel and manganese dissolve, migrate to the graphite and thicken its SEI, and the phase changes at high charge crack the particles and open new surface.2 In one comparison of full cells against graphite, NMC111 and NMC622 cycled stably when charged to 4.4 V and NMC811 only to 4.0 V; above those limits the impedance of the positive electrode grew, from the point where each began to release oxygen from its surface, about 4.7 V vs Li for NMC111 and NMC622 and about 4.3 V for NMC811. The best stable result came from NMC622 charged to 4.4 V, about 650 mWh per gram of NMC (figure 13.1).4 Nickel-rich materials are also less thermally stable and more sensitive to air and moisture, which makes them harder and costlier to manufacture.1,3

Worked example. What does a higher cut-off buy, and what does it cost? Take NMC622 in the full cells of figure 9.12. to 4.2 V: 155.4 mAh/g at cycle 5, 147.3 at cycle 300: kept 147.3/155.4= 95 % to 4.4 V: 177.8 → 166.8 mAh/g: 166.8/155.4=1.07, still 7 % more than the 4.2 V cell gave at cycle 5 to 4.6 V: 203.1 → 79.9 mAh/g: kept 79.9/203.1= 39 % Each extra 0.2 V first buys capacity; past the oxygen-release point it destroys it.

Which material wins depends on the use. Nickel-rich NMC and NCA serve long-range and high-performance cars; LFP, cheaper and longer-lived, has come back strongly, its uptake in electric vehicles doubling since 2020, and it serves grid storage and shorter-range cars.1 Cell and pack design have narrowed its energy gap: state-of-the-art LFP cells rose from 90 Wh/kg in 2010 to 160 Wh/kg in 2022, against about 300 Wh/kg for cells with NMC-type positive electrodes.1

What comes next

Several routes lead on. Single-crystal NMC particles avoid the cracks between primary grains that shorten the life of nickel-rich materials.1 Manganese-rich chemistries balance cost against range: LMFP, LiFePO4 with manganese added, reaches cells of 210 to 240 Wh/kg; LNMO works at about 4.7 V but waits for an electrolyte that survives there; lithium- and manganese-rich NMC offers about 280 mAh/g but loses voltage as it cycles.1,2 In 2023, none of these high-manganese cells had clearly passed technology readiness level 5.5 Conversion materials such as FeF3 and FeS2 promise 700 to 900 mAh/g at 1 to 2.5 V vs Li, at the price of large hysteresis, sluggish kinetics and strain.5 And molecules can take the transition metal out altogether, which is where we end this module.

A positive electrode is a compromise between capacity, voltage, stability and cost; more nickel buys capacity and spends margin.
