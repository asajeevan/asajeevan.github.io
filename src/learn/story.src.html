Battery basics, from zero

How batteries work

Where the push in a battery comes from, and how much of it there is: from two charges in empty space to the energy in your phone’s cell.

I spent my doctoral years building cells like the ones on this page. This is the explanation I wish I had had at the start: five modules, from charge and voltage to capacity and energy, each built around a figure you can play with and told in the order the play raises the questions. Further modules, following one ion through a cycle and into the ways cells age, are being written. Every factual statement was checked against a textbook or a peer-reviewed article, listed at the end; every curve is computed from the equation in its source, and every figure can show you what it is based on. Nothing here is experimental data.

“Just show me” keeps the questions, the figures and the one-line answers. “I want the equations” opens every “Go deeper” panel and the mathematics of each module.

Where this leaves you

You started with two charges in empty space and ended with the energy of a cell. In between you saw why a voltage exists before anything is connected, where inside the cell it sits, how the parts are built and why, what sets the voltage of a pair of materials and the ceiling the electrolyte puts on it, and how Faraday’s constant turns grams into ampere-hours and volts into watt-hours. Your cell, Graphite | LiCoO2, is remembered by this page.

What comes next is being written: the voltage curve a cell draws as it runs, the lithium ion’s journey out of one host, across the electrolyte and into the other, the composite electrode it has to find its way into, and the ways in which all of that goes wrong as a cell ages. It will appear here, module by module, each one checked against its sources in the same way.
