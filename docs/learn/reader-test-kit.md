# Reader-test kit for the battery pages (plan, section 5c)

Prepared 2026-10-01. Nothing here has been run yet. The test needs three real readers and the
owner, and its results go at the end of this file.

## Who and how

- **Three readers:**
  - one with no science background (reader A);
  - one undergraduate in a science or engineering subject (reader B);
  - one battery colleague (reader C).
- **Reading mode:** each reader reads the page at their own level.
  - Reader A uses "Just show me" mode, then opens the explanations of any module they want.
  - Reader B uses "Explain it".
  - Reader C uses "I want the equations".
- **Phone and desktop:** at least one reader uses a phone. The figures are drawn for 360 px and
  above; ask that reader whether they could read the labels without zooming (see "Known limit"
  below).
- **No help while reading.** The reader may stop at any time; note where.
- **Afterwards**, each reader:
  - answers the five core questions below, in their own words, without looking back;
  - names the module where they got lost and the one where they got bored (either may be "none");
  - names the figure that helped most and the one that confused most.

## Acceptance (from the plan)

- All three readers finish the page.
- Reader A answers at least three of the five core questions acceptably.
- Nobody names the same module as boring twice.
- A module that fails is re-paced, not cut.

## The five core questions (one per stage of the page)

1. **Module 0 and 1:** Why does a battery have a voltage even when nothing is connected to it, and
   why do the electrons go round through the wire while the ions go through the liquid?
2. **Module 3 and 4:** Why do lithium cells have higher voltages than, say, an alkaline cell, and
   why is a cell's energy the area under its voltage curve rather than just its voltage?
3. **Module 6 and 7:** Where is the lithium in a charged lithium-ion cell, and where is it in a
   discharged one? On its way between the two, what is the lithium ion surrounded by?
4. **Module 5 and 8:** Why does a cell give less voltage while it is working than when it rests,
   and why does a cell charged fast get less full?
5. **Module 10 and 12:** Name one reason a battery slowly loses capacity, and one thing a user
   can do that the page says helps.

**Acceptable-answer notes, for the owner.** These are drawn from the page, not a rubric to read
out.

- **Q1.** The two electrodes hold their electrons at different energies, so there is a potential
  difference across the cell. Electrons cannot cross the electrolyte, which carries only ions,
  so they must go through the wire.
- **Q2.**
  - Lithium is the most strongly reducing electrode, and organic electrolytes survive voltages at
    which water would be split.
  - Energy is charge times voltage summed along the curve. The voltage changes as the cell
    discharges, so what counts is the area.
- **Q3.**
  - Charged: mostly between the graphite sheets.
  - Discharged: between the oxide layers of the positive electrode.
  - In the liquid the ion carries a shell of about four solvent molecules (and sometimes an anion).
- **Q4.** Resistance and the slowness of reactions and of diffusion take voltage away under
  current. Charging faster means the voltage limit is reached earlier.
- **Q5.**
  - Reasons for loss: the SEI keeps consuming lithium; particles crack or lose contact;
    manganese dissolves; lithium plates.
  - User advice, which the page labels as its own reading of the sources: keep the cell cool,
    avoid leaving it full for long, and do not fast-charge it in the cold.

- they used pinch-zoom;
- they turned the phone sideways.
